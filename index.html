Research

Adversarial safety evaluation for relationship-forming AI

The H Programme is an empirical research programme investigating how AI companions can be attacked and manipulated across sustained multi-session interactions. The infrastructure includes an AWS-hosted mentorship platform using Claude as the mentor model, Amazon Titan embeddings for persistent memory, three culturally grounded AI mentor personas with full 24-facet HEXACO personality profiles, and a five-model judge panel drawn from four model families.

What safety scores miss: four architectural requirements for multi-axis evaluation of relational AI Intended submission ▶
Regulators across five jurisdictions have, since 2023, enacted binding safety obligations on artificial intelligence systems designed for sustained, personalised, or anthropomorphic interaction with users. The obligations name the harms, but none of the instruments specifies the evaluation methodology by which compliance is to be demonstrated. The 2026 International AI Safety Report identifies the resulting gap as structural rather than temporary. Scalar safety scoring, the default across contemporary pre-deployment evaluation, was developed for single turn tasks with categorical outputs and cannot detect harms that accumulate across the trajectory of a conversation. This paper develops a normative architectural specification for what can. Drawing on trait psychology and on contemporary work on persona representation in large language models, it derives four joint requirements that any compliance adequate evaluation of relational AI must satisfy. The first concerns how scoring is conditioned on the context producing the behaviour. The second concerns how variance is partitioned across the interaction terms that conditioning produces. The third concerns how the integrity of the persona is tracked separately from safety. The fourth concerns how the identity of the judge is reported as a first class component of the evaluation result. The requirements are joint rather than independent, each necessary for the validity of the others. They are offered as a candidate specification for the standards work currently underway across the European Union, China, the United States, and the United Kingdom, where binding compliance methodology for relational AI safety obligations is yet to be codified.
Same Persona, Different Pressure: A Disordinal Interaction in Persona-Conditioned Affective AI Intended submission ▶
Persona conditioning in large language models has emerged as a high variance behavioural control with documented consequences for the safety of sustained one to one interactions in companion, coaching, mentoring, and other relational roles. Single turn benchmark evaluation has begun to characterise how persona shapes safety outcomes; multi turn relational evaluation has not. This study tests whether the same psychological trait, instantiated in the same persona, can produce directionally opposite safety expression across categorically distinct adversarial contexts under sustained multi turn pressure. The configuration tested is HEXACO calibrated mentor AI; the architectural question is general to persona conditioned affective AI. Three mentor personas were exposed to four categories of adversarial pressure generated by seven user simulations, with each conversation independently scored on two axes by a cross family panel of four large language model judges. The principal finding is a disordinal mentor by attack category interaction. The rank ordering of the three personas reverses across the four categories, with formal inferential support across the Aligned Rank Transform, permutation testing, and ordinal logistic regression, and with robustness across all seven user simulations. A co equal observation concerns the divergence between category specific and cross category safety scoring on the same conversations. Parasocial Acceleration conversations receive catastrophic category specific scores from the same judges who assign near ceiling cross category scores and classify 97.6 percent of records as showing no observable attack success. The implication is that aggregate safety performance and holistic scoring are inadequate proxies for category specific risk.
